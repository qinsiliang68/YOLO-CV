## Research Question

Can the effect of a training example on a target example be attributed along the actual optimization path, and can that attribution be approximated cheaply from saved checkpoints and selected layers?

## Three Distinct Quantities

### Ideal path attribution

If training example `z_t` is used at update `t`, exact ideal influence on target `z'` is the target-loss change caused at that update:

```text
TracInIdeal(z, z') = sum_{t: z_t=z} [L(theta_t, z') - L(theta_{t+1}, z')]
```

Summing over all training samples telescopes exactly to total target-loss reduction from initialization to the final model. This is an accounting identity along one realized training path, not an intrinsic property of `z`.

### First-order step attribution

For SGD update `theta_{t+1} = theta_t - eta_t g_t`, a small-step Taylor approximation gives:

```text
L(theta_t, z') - L(theta_{t+1}, z')
  approximately eta_t * grad L(theta_t, z') dot grad L(theta_t, z_t)
```

For a mini-batch of size `b`, the paper assigns one `1/b` share to each sample's gradient-dot term. The batch's first-order sum is defined, but attribution among batch members is an additive convention; interaction and curvature are not identified.

### Checkpoint heuristic

With checkpoints `theta_c`, the practical score is:

```text
TracInCP(z, z') = sum_c eta_c * grad L(theta_c, z) dot grad L(theta_c, z')
```

This does not replay the actual moment at which `z` was used. The paper explicitly offers a counterfactual interpretation: the sample is treated as if it had been visited at every supplied checkpoint. It can even score a point that was never in training. Therefore `TracInCP` is a state-conditioned similarity/influence heuristic, not exact historical causation.

## Impact Versus Value

Self-influence sets `z'=z`:

```text
self_influence(z) = sum_c eta_c * ||grad L(theta_c, z)||^2
```

It is nonnegative and mostly measures persistent leverage/memorization or outlier behavior. It cannot say whether replay helps Stage1's normal tail or harms its weak-defect tail.

Target influence retains direction:

```text
target_influence(z, T) = sum_c eta_c * grad L(theta_c, z) dot grad L_target(theta_c, T)
```

Positive first-order alignment means an SGD step on `z` locally reduces that chosen target loss. It still does not guarantee finite-step improvement under large aggregate replay, curvature, momentum, adaptive preconditioning or target mismatch.

## Experimental Contract

### CIFAR-10

- ResNet-56, 50,000 training samples, 270 epochs, batch 1000.
- Initial learning rate 0.1, 15-epoch warmup, multipliers changing at epochs 90, 180 and 240.
- Ten percent of labels are changed to the model's highest-scoring incorrect label, not random label noise.
- The noisy model has 99.6% train accuracy and 87.0% test accuracy versus 93.4% clean test accuracy.
- TracInCP uses only the final layer and every 30th epoch checkpoint starting at epoch 30.
- More than 80% of synthetic mislabels are reported within the first 20% of the TracIn ranking, while two comparators recover below 50% there.
- Equal checkpoint weighting is reported as similar in this one experiment.

### MNIST

- Three-hidden-layer network with about 240K parameters.
- Exact per-step first-order TracIn and all-parameter variants are feasible.
- The reported correlation between batch first-order influence and actual target-loss change is 0.978 over updates for 100 test points.
- A middle checkpoint correlates better with exact TracIn than the final checkpoint; more checkpoints and high-loss-reduction checkpoints improve correlation.
- Influence-function computation sometimes fails to converge and is noisy; lower learning rates and larger batches are used to mitigate this, increasing cost.

### Applications

- California housing: three-layer regression model, 200 epochs, every 20th checkpoint.
- DBPedia: SWEM, 60 epochs, six evenly spaced checkpoints, final fully connected layer.
- ImageNet: ResNet-50, 90 epochs, checkpoints 30/60/90, final fully connected layer and projected gradient dimension 1472 in the paper.
- The application section is explicitly qualitative rather than a quantitative evaluation.

The paper does not report a paired multi-seed uncertainty analysis for the central rankings or a same-selection replay experiment.

## Main Results And Mechanism Evidence

1. Gradient direction across a target and training sample contains information that gradient norm alone cannot provide.
2. Different checkpoints identify different mislabeled classes; influence is training-stage dependent.
3. Checkpoints with active loss reduction are more informative than a final converged checkpoint with tiny gradients.
4. A single final checkpoint can be noisy; aggregation across useful states matters.
5. Random projections provide an unbiased dot-product estimator in expectation, with variance decreasing as projection dimension grows.
6. For a fully connected layer, the weight-gradient inner product factorizes into output-error similarity times activation similarity, enabling cheap final-layer probes.
7. Near-duplicate samples expose a conceptual difference: deleting one copy can show little influence even when the duplicate group is collectively influential. Set/context identity matters.

## Ablations, Assumptions And Failure Cases

### Replay violates the one-visit checkpoint assumption

The approximate telescoping intuition assumes every training example is visited once between epoch-boundary checkpoints. Stage1 deliberately visits replay samples extra times. A replay-aware estimator must retain actual occurrence/exposure counts or explicitly use a counterfactual score; it cannot silently apply the one-visit interpretation.

### Adaptive optimizers are not derived

The paper derives the dot product for plain SGD and says other optimizers require the update formula to be changed. It expects TracInCP's form to remain useful but does not prove this for Adam/AdamW, momentum, weight decay or Ultralytics `optimizer=auto`.

For Stage1, two signals must remain separate:

```text
raw_gradient_alignment = grad_target dot grad_sample
actual_update_alignment = -grad_target dot delta_theta_actual
```

The second includes optimizer state and is closer to the realized step, but attribution to one sample in a mixed mini-batch with momentum remains approximate.

### Positive alignment is local

The 0.978 MNIST correlation is specific to small steps, one model and selected settings. The neglected Taylor remainder grows with update norm and curvature. Repeating an aligned sample can overshoot or become stale.

### Self-influence mixes noise and useful hardness

The synthetic mislabel experiment validates outlier detection under a constructed corruption process. High self-influence is not positive business value; it can mean mislabeled, memorized or isolated data.

### Target sign depends on the target's correctness

The authors intentionally use neutral `proponent` and `opponent` terminology because the semantic value reverses when the explained target is mislabeled. Stage1 target probes must therefore be label-audited and split into difficult-normal and weak-defect objectives.

### Layer and checkpoint cherry-picking changes the quantity

Final-layer influence is cheap but omits representation changes. Equal weighting and three checkpoints worked in selected cases, but no universal agreement bound against full-network, all-step influence is supplied.

### Central code is incomplete

The public repository contains the ImageNet application notebooks but not the CIFAR-10/MNIST comparison code used for the main quantitative figures. Exact reruns of those claims are not possible from the repository alone.

## Code Audit

- The proponents/opponents notebook uses checkpoints 30, 60 and 90, factorizing the final-layer weight-gradient inner product into error and activation similarities.
- It defines `one_hot - probability` rather than the usual cross-entropy logit gradient `probability - one_hot`; because both sides use the same sign, their dot product is unchanged.
- It explicitly ignores final-layer bias.
- The self-influence notebook watches the final kernel and bias, then sums squared per-example Jacobians across the three checkpoints.
- Both notebooks use equal checkpoint weights rather than `eta_c`.
- The notebooks alternate `TracIn` and `TrackIn` spellings and use mutable Colab path parameters.
- There is no environment lock, test suite, sample/checkpoint hash manifest, atomic output protocol or numerical cross-check against Captum/another implementation.
- The proponents notebook says a 10% ImageNet subset fits a 12 GB Colab; full ImageNet needs around 40 GB RAM. The self-influence notebook claims roughly 15 minutes over 1.28M samples on TPU, but no machine manifest or timing artifact is provided.

## What It Supports For Stage1

1. Collect direction, not only gradient norm.
2. Compute influence against separate difficult-normal and weak-defect probe losses.
3. Preserve checkpoint-specific values and sign changes; do not immediately collapse them into one scalar.
4. Sample gradients during active learning phases and avoid assuming the final checkpoint is representative.
5. Use last-layer factorization or calibrated random projections as low-cost mechanism probes.
6. Record actual replay occurrence, cumulative exposure and checkpoint interval counts.
7. Compare raw-gradient and optimizer-aware update alignment under the canonical optimizer.
8. Treat duplicate/video groups and set-level influence separately from single-image influence.
9. Keep target probes in OOF/val_op discovery data and leave blind test sealed.

## What It Does Not Support

1. It does not support `large gradient = valuable sample`.
2. It does not support `positive TracInCP = replay will improve final metrics`.
3. It does not validate additive replay, dynamic decay, weak-defect guard or an FN-constrained frontier.
4. It does not provide a Stage1 replay fraction, checkpoint list, decay start or guard share.
5. It does not prove raw-gradient TracIn under AdamW or momentum.
6. It does not show cross-seed stability of a frozen selected set.
7. It does not establish last-layer/full-network agreement for this task.
8. It does not justify fitting influence targets on the final test set.

## Transfer Boundary And Field Contract

At each low-cost epoch, retain ordinary training telemetry and replay exposure. At selected gradient checkpoints, collect:

- `sample_grad_norm_last_layer`;
- `sample_grad_dot_normal_tail` and cosine;
- `sample_grad_dot_weak_defect_tail` and cosine;
- projected or factorized gradient embedding plus projection seed/hash;
- `base_grad`, `replay_grad` and combined residual-to-target summaries;
- sign and rank stability across checkpoints and seeds;
- current learning rate, optimizer family/state identity and actual parameter-delta norm;
- `actual_update_dot_normal_tail` and `actual_update_dot_weak_defect_tail` on controlled probe steps;
- sample occurrence count and cumulative replay exposure between checkpoints;
- checkpoint SHA, initial-weight SHA and canonical hyperparameter-lock SHA.

Do not store an unqualified field named `influence`. Use explicit names such as:

```text
tracin_cp_rawgrad_lastlayer_normal_tail
tracin_cp_rawgrad_lastlayer_defect_tail
actual_update_alignment_normal_tail
actual_update_alignment_defect_tail
```

## Concrete Experiment Consequence

- Gradient capture remains a diagnostic channel in the first campaign, not an adaptive intervention that changes training.
- At a small number of checkpoint states, compare last-layer and a sampled fuller-network projection to quantify approximation error.
- For identical replay selections that reverse across seeds, test whether checkpoint-wise target-alignment signs, optimizer-aware update alignment or exposure-weighted sums reverse with them.
- Keep replay timing/dose arms causal and frozen; use influence trajectories to explain outcomes after the block rather than tune arms mid-block.
- Preserve canonical hyperparameters exactly. A hyperparameter-lock mismatch invalidates influence comparisons because both the path and checkpoint gradients are conditional on that configuration.
