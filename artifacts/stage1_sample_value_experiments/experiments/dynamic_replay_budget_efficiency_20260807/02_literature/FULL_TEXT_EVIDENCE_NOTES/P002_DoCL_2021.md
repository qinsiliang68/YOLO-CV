## Research Question

Can curriculum selection be derived from the model's current learning dynamics rather than from a static notion of easy, hard, or representative data? The paper asks which subset at time `t` most rapidly moves predictions over the data distribution toward their targets.

## Method And Equations

For regression, the per-step objective chooses a subset whose induced output dynamics have maximum projection onto the residual over the data distribution:

```text
max_S E_{x~D} <y - f_t(x), df_t(x)/dt |_S>
```

The paper rearranges this into a per-sample score. Its key regression score (Eq. 7) is:

```text
a_t(i) = <y_i - f_t(x_i), df_t(x_i)/dt |_D>
```

For classification, the residual becomes the one-hot residual `y_i - p_t(x_i)`, while the dynamics are estimated on the pre-softmax outputs under a large-batch L2 update (Eq. 12):

```text
a_t(i) = <y_i - p_t(x_i), df_t(x_i)/dt |_D>
```

The actual selected mini-batch is trained with cross-entropy. Therefore the score-estimation update and the ordinary task update use different losses in the classification derivation. This distinction matters for reproduction.

The score combines two quantities:

1. residual magnitude and sign, indicating what remains unlearned;
2. speed and direction of the sample output under learning induced by the data distribution.

It is therefore neither gradient norm nor loss alone. A high-loss sample whose prediction does not move in a useful direction receives less value than a high-loss sample whose output is moving consistently toward its target.

The NTK interpretation (Eq. 14) makes context dependence explicit. With residual `r` and kernel/gradient-similarity matrix `H`, the contribution of sample `i` contains:

```text
r_i * sum_j H_ij * r_j / |D|
```

Thus the score changes with model state and depends on residuals of related samples. The paper argues that this rejects two simplistic groups: difficult but weakly related outliers, and already easy samples whose residuals are too small to improve difficult samples.

Because only selected samples receive fresh observations, DoCL maintains an exponential moving average of scores (Eq. 8). Although the derivation suggests top-k selection, the practical algorithm uses score-weighted sampling for exploration. It can additionally prune by facility-location diversity in last-layer feature space.

## Algorithmic Contract

Algorithm 1 couples several mechanisms:

- warm-start epochs over the full dataset using large-batch SGD and L2 loss to initialize scores;
- repeated episodes/cycles with cosine learning-rate restarts;
- weighted sampling proportional to the moving score rather than deterministic top-k;
- task training on selected data with mini-batch SGD and task loss;
- periodic large-batch full-data updates to refresh score estimates;
- optional facility-location diversity;
- multiplicative reduction in selected-set size after each cycle, bounded at `k_min = 0.2n`;
- default reduction and EMA-related factors around 0.9, selected from `{0.85, 0.9, 0.95}` in reported experiments;
- default warm start `T0 = 5` and temperature `tau = 0.1` in the main ablation.

This is not a clean single-factor test of late-stage replay. Score, exploration, diversity, subset size, learning-rate cycles, and periodic full-data updates are coupled.

## Experimental Contract

The paper trains from scratch on nine image datasets:

- WideResNet-28-10 on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100;
- ResNeXt50-32x4d on Food-101, FGVC Aircraft, Stanford Cars and Birdsnap;
- ResNet-50 on ImageNet;
- WideResNet-16-8 on Fashion-MNIST;
- PreActResNet-34 on SVHN.

Baselines are random mini-batch SGD, self-paced learning, minimax curriculum learning, and DoCL-NR, which removes the residual term and keeps dynamics magnitude. Momentum is 0.9. The methods share a selected cosine-cycle schedule per dataset, standard augmentation, and Mixup with alpha 0.4. Training ranges from 200 epochs on ImageNet to 300 or 400 epochs on other datasets. Supplement Table 2 reports batch sizes, learning-rate endpoints and weight decay; ImageNet uses batch 256 for 200 epochs, while CIFAR uses batch 128 for 300 epochs.

The paper says several schedules were tried and the best validation schedule was then shared across methods. It also says several subset factors were tried and the best was chosen. These are relevant tuning details, not preregistered constants transferable to Stage1.

## Main Results

Table 1 reports DoCL as the highest final test accuracy on all nine datasets. Examples include:

- CIFAR-10: DoCL 97.43 versus random 96.18;
- CIFAR-100: 83.23 versus 79.64;
- ImageNet: 79.54 versus 75.04;
- Birdsnap: 71.37 versus 64.23;
- Stanford Cars: 86.26 versus 78.73.

The training curves show that the method is not uniformly best at every early checkpoint. On CIFAR-10 it improves more slowly early and overtakes later. This is direct evidence that intermediate dynamics and final outcome need not have the same ordering.

## Ablations And Failure Cases

Figure 5 varies diversity pruning, cyclical versus exponential learning rate, warm-start length, temperature, subset reduction, and score averaging. The authors report modest sensitivity for most settings, with diversity, cyclical schedules, longer warm start, lower temperature, and moderate reduction performing slightly better. A two-epoch warm start performs much worse early because exploration is insufficient, although it later approaches other variants.

The paper's own theory also identifies a failure case for hardness-only selection: a high-residual sample can be an outlier or adversarially chosen if it has weak or conflicting relation to the rest of the data. High impact is therefore not equivalent to positive value.

## What It Supports For Stage1

1. Sample value is conditional on model state and training time, not only an intrinsic scalar attached to an image.
2. Residual or confidence should be combined with direction/speed of learning and cross-sample consistency.
3. A time-smoothed score can be more meaningful than a one-shot score.
4. Exploration and weighted sampling are safer hypotheses than repeatedly taking a deterministic static top-k.
5. Diversity can be applied after a target-relevant candidate pool is defined.
6. Early broad exposure followed by reduced selected exposure is a literature-supported hypothesis worth testing.
7. Training trajectories must be observed, because the ordering of methods can change during training.

## What It Does Not Support

1. It does not study adding duplicate replay samples while retaining every base-training sample each epoch.
2. It does not optimize `FN <= 95`, a raw-score safety frontier, normal high-tail, or defect low-tail.
3. It does not test a weak-defect guard.
4. It does not test identical selections across many initialization seeds or report Stage1-like cross-seed reversals.
5. It does not justify stopping replay at epoch 140, 160, or any Stage1-specific checkpoint.
6. It does not justify replay ratios of 0.5%, 1.0%, 2.5%, or any other Stage1 percentage.
7. Its final table does not establish paired cross-seed success probability or a confidence bound for operational safety.
8. Its coupled algorithm does not isolate whether gains come from score, schedule, total dose, exploration, diversity, or learning-rate restarts.

## Transfer Boundary

Stage1 always trains on the complete base pool and adds replay exposure. DoCL instead chooses a time-varying subset for ordinary training. Therefore the paper transfers at the level of mechanism hypotheses and field requirements, not at the level of directly copying its algorithm or constants.

The useful transfer is:

```text
conditional value = residual x learning direction x sample context x time
```

The unsafe transfer would be:

```text
DoCL reduces subset size, therefore Stage1 must stop replay late
```

That causal conclusion requires our own dose-matched and time-matched experiment.

## Concrete Field Requirements

For a lightweight all-epoch Stage1 analogue, capture at every epoch:

- sample ID, draw role and cumulative replay exposure;
- label, raw logit and class probability;
- residual to the label;
- change in logit/probability since the previous observation;
- learning-rate sum over the observation interval;
- learning-rate-normalized output change;
- exponential moving averages and sign consistency of the above;
- current selection probability or replay weight;
- model seed, checkpoint epoch, schedule arm and sample-set identity.

At the preregistered heavy checkpoints, capture last-layer gradient norm and alignment to fixed normal-tail and defect-tail probes. That extension is motivated by Stage1's target, not claimed as a DoCL result.

## Concrete Experiment Consequence

- Preserve a continuous-replay arm, but compare it with a schedule that reduces replay exposure later.
- Include a cumulative-dose-matched schedule so timing is not confused with total exposure.
- Keep selection fixed inside this first causal comparison; otherwise dynamic selection and schedule cannot be separated.
- Add a later dynamic-score arm only after the schedule mechanism is identified.
- Do not use deterministic static top-k as the only implementation; retain a preregistered weighted/exploratory candidate for a later transfer test.
- Report every epoch's lightweight trajectory and key-checkpoint raw predictions rather than only the final point.
