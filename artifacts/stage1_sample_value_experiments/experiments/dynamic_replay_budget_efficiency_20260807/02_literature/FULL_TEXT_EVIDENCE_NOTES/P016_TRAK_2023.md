## Research Question

Can a small ensemble of linearized, randomly projected gradient-kernel estimators predict counterfactual model behavior with much less retraining than empirical datamodels?

## Evaluation Target: LDS

For a target `z`, an attribution method supplies a vector over training examples. The paper assumes a subset's predicted output is the sum of the selected entries:

```text
g_tau(z, S') = sum_(i in S') tau(z)_i
```

The linear datamodeling score is the Spearman correlation, over random training subsets, between this additive prediction and the average output of models actually retrained on each subset.

The main LDS ground truth uses:

- 100 random subsets, each containing 50% of the training data;
- five independently trained models per subset;
- up to 2,000 validation queries;
- 95% bootstrap intervals from resampling subsets.

These intervals quantify subset/query uncertainty, not success probability over future training seeds. LDS also bakes in individual-score additivity and is not a direct replay-value metric.

## TRAK Estimator

For multiclass classification, the model output is the correct-class log odds:

```text
f(z; theta) = log(p_correct / (1 - p_correct))
```

The model is linearized at a trained checkpoint, and its output gradient is randomly projected:

```text
phi(z) = P^T grad_theta f(z; theta*)
Phi = stack_i phi(z_i)
Q_ii = 1 - p_correct(z_i; theta*)
```

The simplified single-model estimator is:

```text
tau(z) = phi(z)^T (Phi^T Phi)^-1 Phi^T Q
```

The theoretically motivated logistic-regression curvature weights and leverage denominator are omitted because ablations show worse or unchanged LDS. The reweighting inverse and `Q` term are empirically important; raw gradient similarity is much weaker.

TRAK averages information from models trained on different random data subsets, then optionally soft-thresholds scores. Projection dimension, ensemble size and threshold are empirical hyperparameters, not universal constants.

## Experimental Contract

- CIFAR-10 uses ResNet-9 trained only 24 epochs with cyclic learning rate; ImageNet uses modified ResNet-18 trained 15 epochs.
- QNLI changes the ordinary BERT fine-tuning recipe: it uses SGD and removes the last tanh specifically to improve LDS.
- Main projection dimensions range from 1,000 to 20,000; model ensemble sizes range from 5 to 100 in central results.
- Soft-thresholding is used for CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. CIFAR-10 chooses sparsity by cross-validation on the same attribution models; ImageNet fixes 1,000 nonzero entries heuristically.
- Datamodel baselines use up to 300,000 trained models; TRAK uses far fewer but is not a one-model method in its strongest form.
- Full-gradient eNTK features materially outperform penultimate/last-layer features at 100-model ensemble size.
- Runtime comparisons use A100 40GB GPUs and extrapolate some influence-function costs from only 50-100 targets.

## Main Results

### Seed/model aggregation is essential

LDS rises substantially as independently trained models are added. The paper explicitly states that behavior caused only by the random seed cannot be explained by training-data identity. This supports retaining a conditional value per seed and separately estimating its population distribution.

The result does not mean seed reversal can be averaged away. A mean score can hide a candidate that helps half the initializations and harms the rest, which is precisely Stage1's safety problem.

### Checkpoints can partly substitute for models

On CIFAR-10, 100 checkpoints assembled from fewer independent trajectories approach the LDS of 100 independent final models. Early checkpoints also retain useful signal, although the reported LDS increases monotonically from epoch 1 to 25.

This is evidence that checkpoint state contains complementary information, not proof that checkpoints from one trajectory equal independent initialization replicates. Stage1 should measure both checkpoint variation and seed variation.

### Full gradient context matters

At 100-model ensemble size, full eNTK TRAK obtains LDS 0.516/0.413/0.589 on CIFAR-2/CIFAR-10/QNLI, versus 0.198/0.120/0.195 using penultimate-layer features. A last-layer gradient probe can be useful and cheap, but its agreement with fuller gradients must be calibrated on a small pool before being treated as sufficient.

### Projection is not monotone

Increasing projection dimension first helps and then hurts LDS. The authors interpret lower-dimensional projection as regularization. Therefore no projection dimension can be imported into Stage1 based only on accuracy preservation arguments.

### Counterfactual behavior differs from semantic support

In fact tracing, lexical retrieval better finds annotated proponents, but removing TRAK-selected data changes model behavior more. This cleanly separates semantic similarity from causal model behavior. It supports our rejection of visually similar or confidence-hard frames as automatically high-value.

## Failure Modes And Limitations

1. TRAK is a checkpoint-local linearization and depends on suitability of the empirical NTK approximation.
2. The paper says early feature-learning dynamics are not modeled; mini-batches, momentum and weight decay are named as future-work omissions.
3. Training-set subset effects are predicted additively even though finite sets can interact.
4. Model and subset ensembling improve the estimate but can conceal heterogeneous signs.
5. Main benchmark training recipes are short or altered to improve attribution evaluation; they do not match Stage1's 200-epoch canonical path.
6. Full-gradient superiority makes a universal last-layer proxy unsupported.
7. Soft-thresholding and projection dimension are selected against LDS and are not reusable business-value thresholds.
8. Removal-and-retraining experiments do not establish benefit from additive duplicate replay.
9. No experiment uses an asymmetric `FN <= 95` safety objective or weak-defect guard.

## Reproduction And Code Audit

- The current repository has useful unit/integration tests and downloadable CIFAR correlation checks, but it differs substantially from the paper-time code. Results must name the commit.
- Persistent metadata checks only JL dimension, projection type and training-set size. It does not bind sample IDs/order, checkpoint/model/task/output function, projection seed, gradient scope, dtype or regularization.
- Training featurization uses an index completion mask, but target scoring has no analogous mask. Interrupted target scoring can leave zero rows that finalization treats as data.
- JSON state writes and memory-map finalization are not atomic or hash-verified. There is no durable dependency graph proving that a finalized score came from complete source and target arrays.
- The score computer directly inverts `Phi^T Phi`, defaults to zero regularization and records neither condition number nor solve residual. It also normalizes each inverse by mean absolute magnitude, which changes relative weighting across ensemble members.
- Finalization accumulates the full train-by-target score matrix in CPU RAM and has no explicit guard for zero completed models before division.
- The paper's soft-thresholding step is absent from the released core package, so the main CIFAR-10/ImageNet LDS pipeline is not reproduced by calling `TRAKer` alone.
- Dependencies are broad and downloadable benchmark artifacts lack hashes in the package metadata.

## Direct Support For Stage1

1. Keep per-seed mechanism scores and report cross-seed mean, dispersion, sign agreement and worst case separately.
2. Use multiple checkpoints as state observations, not as a claim that independent seed replication is unnecessary.
3. Compare last-layer and fuller-gradient probes on a small stratified pool before selecting the cheap scope.
4. Include a contextual reweighting or residual-correction diagnostic; raw gradient magnitude/cosine is not enough.
5. Use exact sample-ID manifests and fixed target-tail manifests. Array position alone is unsafe.
6. Stream candidates against a small normal-tail and weak-defect query set; never materialize 120k-by-120k scores.
7. Persist projection seed/dimension/type, gradient scope, numerical regularization, condition diagnostics and code commit.
8. Keep timing/dose/guard/no-replay arms as actual causal interventions. TRAK remains an observational predictor until calibrated against finite replay.
9. Do not optimize projection, threshold or output function on blind holdout.

## What It Does Not Support

1. A fixed static TRAK ranking as Stage1 replay value.
2. Averaging across seeds and discarding harmful conditional outcomes.
3. Treating 100 checkpoints from one trajectory as 100 unseen-seed confirmations.
4. Assuming last-layer alignment is faithful without calibration.
5. Reusing its projection dimensions, soft-threshold sparsity or training epochs as Stage1 hyperparameters.
6. Claiming subset-removal causality transfers to repeated replay.
7. Calling LDS correlation a safety metric or success probability.

## Stage1 Field Contract

Persist for any projected-gradient attribution probe:

- code commit, estimator equation variant and output-function definition;
- model/checkpoint/initialization/data/hyperparameter-lock hashes and resolved optimizer;
- exact candidate and target sample IDs, labels, fold, row order and augmentation/view identity;
- checkpoint epoch, seed, independent-run versus same-trajectory-checkpoint identity;
- projection type, seed, dimension, dtype, gradient scope and random-generator implementation;
- `Q` term, inverse/reweighting variant, regularization, condition number, solve residual and per-model normalization;
- source/target completion masks, partition manifests, row counts, hashes and atomic finalization status;
- per-seed/per-checkpoint scores before aggregation, sign stability and between-state variance;
- separate normal-tail benefit and weak-defect harm outputs;
- finite replay/continuation calibration delta, runtime, CPU/GPU peak memory and failure status.

## Concrete Experiment Consequence

- Do not add TRAK-selected Treatment to the first large GPU cycle.
- Reuse its engineering idea of streamed projected gradients only after adding Stage1 identity, atomicity and resume contracts.
- In the mechanism canary, compare raw last-layer alignment, projected contextual alignment and actual finite replay effects on the same stratified candidate pool.
- Promote a projected-gradient score only if it predicts unseen seed-state intervention signs and weak-defect harm beyond simple trajectory baselines.
- Freeze all canonical yolo11l training hyperparameters from actual prior artifacts; attribution-specific projection settings remain diagnostic and cannot alter the causal training arms.
